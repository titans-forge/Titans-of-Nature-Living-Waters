# Titans of Nature: Living Waters

## Product Brief

**Working title:** Titans of Nature: Living Waters  
**Genre:** Educational fishing adventure and ecosystem simulator  
**Audience:** Ages 10 and older  
**Platform:** Local-first browser game, desktop and mobile  
**Session length:** 8-15 minutes per expedition  
**Campaign length:** 5-7 expeditions per region  

Living Waters is a simple global fishing game about learning how water, plants,
animals, climate, and human communities fit together. The player travels with a
field journal rather than a trophy case. Every trip begins by meeting people who
know the water, asking useful questions, selecting appropriate equipment, and
reading the habitat before making the first cast.

The game should provide the anticipation and "one more cast" appeal of a fishing
game without reducing nature to a list of collectible fish. A hooked turtle, a
line tangled in floodplain roots, a sudden fall in dissolved oxygen, or a local
market closing for a spawning season may teach more than the intended catch.

The central promise is:

> Every cast reveals part of a living system.

## Design Principles

1. **The watershed is the main character.** Fish are important, but plants,
   insects, birds, reptiles, water chemistry, seasonal floods, and people all
   shape what happens.
2. **Local knowledge changes play.** Conversations are not decorative lore.
   Asking the right question can reveal a safe channel, spawning closure,
   suitable bait, dangerous weather, or fragile plant bed.
3. **Fishing remains easy to understand.** Observe, prepare, cast, respond, and
   record. Biology adds meaning rather than control complexity.
4. **Unexpected catches create decisions.** Bycatch and snags teach handling,
   habitat, and gear selection. They are not comedy punishments.
5. **No universal moral shortcut.** Catch-and-release, subsistence harvest,
   invasive-species removal, scientific sampling, and closed fishing each make
   sense under different local conditions.
6. **People are experts, not scenery.** Local characters have occupations,
   interests, obligations, disagreements, and reasons to trust or distrust the
   visitor.
7. **Curiosity replaces gambling.** Use anticipation, rapid feedback, rare
   observations, journal completion, and changing conditions, but no wagering,
   loot boxes, artificial near-misses, or paid energy.

## Core Player Loop

### 1. Arrive and meet the community

The player enters a specific place, season, and local situation. A short scene
introduces two or three people, such as a fishing guide, boat pilot, botanist,
market seller, fisheries officer, farmer, or field biologist.

Each person offers several questions. Time allows the player to ask only two or
three before departure. Useful questions include:

- Where did the water rise overnight?
- Which plants mark deep or oxygen-rich water?
- What is spawning here now?
- Which species may be kept, measured, or only observed?
- What gear causes the least trouble in this habitat?
- What changed since the previous season?

Answers are entered in the field journal and become practical hints during the
trip. The player is never tested on information that has not been observed,
asked about, or reasonably inferred.

### 2. Prepare the trip

The player chooses a compact loadout:

- Rod or handline type
- Line strength
- Hook type and size
- Bait or lure
- Landing and release equipment
- Two field tools, such as thermometer, turbidity tube, plant guide, camera,
  dissolved-oxygen meter, or measuring board

Gear has understandable tradeoffs. Heavy line reaches powerful fish but is more
visible. Barbless hooks reduce handling damage. Surface lures avoid submerged
plants but miss bottom-feeding species. A water test occupies space that could
hold another lure.

### 3. Read the water

Before casting, the player inspects a living scene. Visible clues include:

- Current speed and water level
- Shade and temperature
- Water color and turbidity
- Insect activity
- Birds feeding or resting
- Plant type and density
- Bubbles, surface strikes, or moving reeds
- Human activity, boats, nets, farms, or livestock upstream

The player selects a fishing position and states a simple hypothesis, such as
"predators are hunting at the flooded forest edge" or "low oxygen is pushing
fish toward moving water." This is not a formal quiz. It helps the simulation
explain why a cast worked or failed.

### 4. Cast and manage the line

The fishing action should be short and tactile:

- Aim the cast with one pointer or thumb movement.
- Set distance with a simple hold-and-release meter.
- Read line tension through sound, motion, and a clear gauge.
- Reel, pause, or give line based on the animal and obstructions.

Each attempt takes approximately 20-45 seconds. Success depends more on habitat,
gear, observation, and response than reflex speed. An untimed accessibility mode
removes the reaction window.

### 5. Resolve the encounter

Possible results include:

- Target fish
- Non-target fish
- Juvenile fish that must be released quickly
- Turtle or other lawful, plausible bycatch
- Aquatic plant, root, branch, old net, or human debris
- Broken line or damaged gear
- A clear view without a catch, such as spawning behavior or a hunting bird
- A weather or water event that ends the cast

The player chooses how to respond: land carefully, cut the line, use a
de-hooking tool, photograph, measure, release, keep where lawful, remove debris,
or call the local expert. Correct handling protects the animal and earns trust.
Poor handling has visible consequences but triggers instruction rather than a
scolding screen.

### 6. Record what happened

The field journal records:

- Species or object encountered
- Habitat and plant association
- Water conditions
- Gear used
- Diet and food-web relationships
- Reproduction or migration notes
- Human uses and local rules
- What the player inferred correctly or missed
- Source notes and media rights

Every entry adds a node to a visible ecosystem web. The reward is not only a
fish portrait. The player sees how floodplain grass, insects, small fish,
predators, birds, people, and seasonal water connect.

## Progression and Scoring

The player develops four independent records:

| Record | What improves it |
|---|---|
| Fieldcraft | Reading water, selecting suitable gear, and handling the line |
| Biology | Identifying organisms and understanding anatomy and behavior |
| Ecology | Connecting plants, habitat, seasons, water conditions, and food webs |
| Community Trust | Listening, respecting access, sharing observations, and responding to local needs |

There is no single aggregate score that can hide reckless behavior behind a
large catch. Each expedition receives four plain-language grades plus a short
field summary.

Regional progress restores a large illustrated ecosystem panorama:

- Bronze: complete the trip safely
- Silver: make sound observations and handling decisions
- Gold: explain several ecological connections without assistance

The player can always continue. Lower mastery changes the journal and available
support, not access to the next region.

## World Campaign

The first release should contain three complete regions. Later releases can add
the others without changing the engine.

### Region I: Amazon Flooded Forest

**Specific setting:** A researched central Amazon floodplain community rather
than a generic "Amazon level."

**Ecological systems:** Seasonal flood pulse, blackwater and whitewater,
flooded forest, floating and rooted aquatic plants, fruit-eating fish, low
oxygen, forest-river nutrient exchange.

**Possible organisms:** Peacock bass, piranha species, catfish, arapaima as a
protected or managed observation rather than an automatic trophy, river turtle,
caiman observation, kingfishers, insects, and floodplain plants.

**Human questions:** Flood timing, community fishing agreements, market demand,
safe channels, and the difference between subsistence, commercial, and sport
fishing.

**Signature event:** The line enters submerged varzea roots. Pulling hard may
damage the plant, break the line, or leave gear in the habitat. The guide teaches
how experienced fishers read the edge before casting.

### Region II: Okavango Seasonal Delta

**Specific setting:** One researched reach of the Okavango system with named
habitat types and locally reviewed characters.

**Ecological systems:** Seasonal flood arrival, papyrus and reed margins,
channels and floodplain pools, fish dispersal, birds, nutrient movement, and
water shared by people and large animals.

**Possible organisms:** Tigerfish, tilapia, catfish, smaller forage fish,
terrapin, fish eagle, aquatic insects, papyrus, reeds, and water lilies.

**Human questions:** Where floodwater has arrived, which channels are safe,
seasonal fishing methods, wildlife distance, and how falling water concentrates
fish.

**Signature event:** A promising bank is unsafe because of fresh hippopotamus
sign. Fishing knowledge includes knowing when not to approach the water.

### Region III: Mekong and Tonle Sap Flood Pulse

**Ecological systems:** River reversal and seasonal expansion, flooded forest,
fish migration, rice landscapes, sediment, dams, channels, and dry-season
concentration.

**Possible organisms:** Snakehead, climbing perch, barbs, catfish, freshwater
turtle, waterbirds, flooded-forest trees, lotus, rice, and aquatic vegetation.

**Human questions:** Seasonal closures, migration routes, household fishing,
market species, flood timing, and why a large river cannot be understood from a
single bank.

**Signature event:** The player hooks a turtle after choosing bait and hook size
poorly. Safe release and future gear changes matter more than the lost cast.

### Later Regions

- **Alaska salmon watershed:** Ocean-river migration, spawning, bears, forest
  nutrients, temperature, and local harvest systems.
- **Japanese river and estuary:** Ayu ecology, mountain-to-sea connections,
  engineered channels, local fishing traditions, and restoration.
- **Mangrove coast:** Salinity, nursery habitat, roots, crabs, juvenile fish,
  storms, and shoreline protection.
- **Great Lakes tributary:** Invasive species, lamprey control, wetlands,
  industrial recovery, sport fishing, and water monitoring.
- **Coral island lagoon:** Reef, seagrass, mangrove, tides, nursery habitat, and
  fishing pressure. This should follow the freshwater engine rather than expand
  the first release.

## Local Character System

Each region should have four to six recurring people. They are fictional
characters grounded in reviewed occupations and local conditions, not invented
spokespeople for an entire culture.

Each character stores:

- Name, occupation, household role, and individual objective
- Areas of practical expertise
- Topics they will discuss initially
- Information unlocked by trust or shared work
- Disagreements with other characters
- Tasks that benefit the community or research effort
- Dialogue source notes and cultural review status

Example disagreements should be substantive:

- A market seller wants predictable supply while a fisheries officer favors a
  seasonal closure.
- A guide favors a proven channel while a biologist asks the player to sample a
  less productive habitat.
- A farmer values flood-control works while fishers depend on seasonal
  connectivity.

No character simply delivers the game's approved answer. The player learns what
each person knows, what each person needs, and where interests differ.

## Ecology Simulation

The engine should be deterministic and seeded. It does not need to simulate
every organism. It needs to make ecological reasoning legible.

Each fishing opportunity is calculated from:

- Region and site
- Season and time of day
- Water level, temperature, turbidity, oxygen, current, and salinity where relevant
- Plant cover and bottom structure
- Species habitat, diet, activity, and migration traits
- Gear, bait, hook, line, and cast position
- Recent weather and human disturbance
- Information learned from local people

Randomness changes the exact encounter, but it never overrides an impossible
combination. A species cannot appear outside its documented range or habitat.
Rare encounters require appropriate conditions rather than an arbitrary jackpot.

The player may inspect a concise "Why this happened" panel after each cast. It
shows the strongest two or three contributing factors without exposing a wall
of probability calculations.

## Educational Content Model

Every organism entry should include:

- Common and scientific name
- Identification traits
- Native range and habitat
- Diet and food-web role
- Reproduction, migration, or life-cycle notes
- Relationship to plants and water conditions
- Human use or conflict where relevant
- Handling and release guidance from an authoritative regional source
- Conservation status with the date checked
- Common confusions
- Sources and media rights

Plant entries receive equal structural treatment. A plant is not merely a
background decoration. It can provide shade, oxygen, spawning surface, food,
bank stability, obstruction, nursery cover, or evidence of water conditions.

Regional content should use primary research, national fisheries and wildlife
agencies, FishBase, IUCN where appropriate, FAO material, museums, universities,
and locally grounded conservation organizations. Current laws, threatened
status, and safe-handling guidance must be verified shortly before release.

## Presentation

### Main play surface

- Full-bleed, region-specific living habitat
- Water movement, plants, insects, birds, and weather as restrained layered animation
- Compact top bar for time, weather, water level, and trip objective
- Collapsible left field kit for gear and tools
- Central cast surface with stable dimensions
- Right-side guide and journal access on desktop
- Staged full-screen panels on mobile

### Visual progression

Each region receives:

- Arrival panorama
- Fishing-site scene for each principal habitat
- Local meeting scene
- Organism illustrations or accurate cutout renders
- Seasonal variants
- Completed ecosystem panorama showing learned relationships

Visuals must show the actual ecology clearly. Avoid generic jungle, river, or
safari imagery that cannot support observation.

### Sound

- Local water, insect, wind, bird, and boat ambience
- Distinct line, reel, snag, and release cues
- Optional self-hosted music used mainly in camp and journal scenes
- No constant triumphant sound for catching an animal
- Separate ambience and music controls

## Accessibility and Safety

- Pointer, touch, and keyboard controls
- Untimed line-management mode
- Reduced motion and reduced water shimmer
- Captions and visual equivalents for all audio cues
- High-contrast tension and condition indicators
- No reliance on color alone
- Plain-language biology with optional deeper context
- Clear wildlife-handling guidance and no encouragement to imitate dangerous
  encounters without local supervision

## Technical Architecture

Use a separate React, Vite, and TypeScript repository. The simulation must run
without AI, accounts, APIs, telemetry, or internet access.

Suggested public interfaces:

```ts
Region { id, title, locality, seasons, siteIds, characterIds, sourceNotes }
Site { id, regionId, habitat, conditions, plantIds, speciesIds, sceneAssets }
Species { id, commonName, scientificName, traits, habitats, diet, handling, sources }
Plant { id, commonName, scientificName, functions, indicators, habitats, sources }
LocalCharacter { id, regionId, role, objective, expertise, dialogueTopicIds }
DialogueTopic { id, question, answer, gameplayHint, prerequisites, sources }
Gear { id, type, traits, habitatEffects, bycatchEffects }
EcologyCondition { temperature, oxygen, turbidity, waterLevel, current, salinity }
CastResult { seed, siteId, organismId, eventId, causes, handlingOptions }
JournalEntry { subjectId, observations, connections, mastery, sourceVersion }
ExpeditionState { regionId, day, time, trust, gear, conditions, journal, status }
```

Pure deterministic modules should own:

- Seeded encounter generation
- Habitat and species eligibility
- Gear interaction
- Line and handling outcomes
- Ecology explanation generation from structured facts
- Trust and journal progression
- Save migration

Content validation should reject unknown species, impossible site ranges,
missing plant functions, duplicate identifiers, unresolved sources, and unsafe
handling text before production builds can pass.

## Production Sequence

### Phase 1: Research and engine specification

- Select the exact Amazon locality for the vertical slice.
- Define the seasonal water model and ten core ecological variables.
- Author six fish, four plants, two reptiles, four local characters, and twelve
  dialogue topics.
- Create the schema, validator, seeded generator, and save system.
- Commission or create art only after habitat descriptions and species lists are approved.

### Phase 2: Grayscale vertical slice

- Build arrival, conversation, loadout, habitat reading, cast, encounter,
  handling, and journal screens.
- Include one target catch, two non-target catches, one turtle bycatch, two
  plant snags, one debris event, and one weather change.
- Complete one 10-minute expedition without polished art.
- Test whether players understand the loop without instructions from the developer.

### Phase 3: Amazon chapter

- Expand to five to seven expeditions across rising, high, and falling water.
- Add recurring characters, trust, competing objectives, and one community decision.
- Build the ecosystem web and regional ending.
- Produce accurate habitat, organism, and character art.

### Phase 4: Africa and Asia

- Reuse the engine, not the facts or visual composition.
- Build Okavango and Mekong chapters with separate ecological variables,
  fishing practices, local conflicts, safety conditions, and review.
- Introduce cross-region comparisons only after each region stands on its own.

### Phase 5: Release hardening

- Independent biological and cultural review
- Media-rights and source audit
- Mobile, keyboard, reduced-motion, and offline testing
- Deterministic simulation balance tests
- Versioned export/import
- GitHub release and itch.io HTML5 archive

## Vertical-Slice Acceptance Tests

- A new player begins fishing within three minutes.
- At least 80 percent of testers understand that speaking to locals changes the trip.
- At least 75 percent can identify one fish-plant-water relationship after play.
- Players can safely resolve turtle bycatch without trial-and-error harm.
- Players voluntarily inspect at least two journal connections.
- No cast result contradicts habitat, range, season, or selected gear.
- A weak trip remains interesting and educational even without a large catch.
- Mobile controls fit at 390 x 844 with no obscured cast or handling action.
- The complete release runs offline after first load.

## Scope Boundaries for Version One

Do not add:

- Open-world 3D travel
- Hundreds of shallow collectible species
- Online multiplayer or leaderboards
- AI-generated biological facts or dialogue during play
- Real-money systems, energy timers, or daily penalties
- Graphic animal injury
- A universal "release everything" rule detached from local law and ecology
- Dangerous-animal encounters treated as action spectacles

## Recommended First Build

Begin with one Amazon floodplain expedition containing:

1. Two short conversations before departure.
2. Three selectable habitats: open channel, floating vegetation, and flooded forest edge.
3. Six fish species, four plants, and one plausible turtle bycatch.
4. Six compact gear choices.
5. Five cast outcomes with clear ecological causes.
6. A journal web linking at least eight organisms and conditions.
7. One return-to-community scene where the player's observations matter.

This is enough to prove whether the combination of human conversation,
fishing, and ecosystem learning is genuinely compelling before building the
rest of the world.
