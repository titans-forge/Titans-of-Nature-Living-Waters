# Living Waters learner playtest

This is a **proposed protocol, not a completed study**. The targets below are product decisions to test, not validated educational norms or achieved results. No claim of a perfect rating, proven learning gains, or suitability for all ages follows from automated tests.

## Participants and setup

Start with a small facilitated pilot of 6–10 learners across the intended younger audience (approximately 10–13) and older readers. Record age bands and participant codes, not names, in the evaluation sheet. Include both confident and less confident readers and a mix of mouse/touch and keyboard use where possible. Record the mode, reading support, and untimed assistance actually used.

Use one venue per learner for a short session; use a paired research journey in a longer follow-up. Do not require all 15 waters to finish a pilot. Ask learners to think aloud when comfortable. Let difficulty appear before providing help, then record the help rather than treating assisted completion as independent completion.

## Observe four outcomes

| Outcome | Procedure | Proposed first-pilot target |
|---|---|---|
| Comprehension and transfer | Before play, ask for an explanation of the journey's mechanism. After play, use a different place and changed conditions; ask for a prediction, one clue, and one limit. | At least 70% can explain one mechanism and support it with a relevant clue after play. Report individual changes as well as the group count. |
| Delayed retention | After about one week, ask another unfamiliar scenario without first reopening the game. Keep this separate from remembering a species name or an answer's position. | At least 60% still give a sound mechanism and relevant clue. Report the number returning; missing follow-ups are not correct answers. |
| Control clarity and enjoyment | Observe the first expedition, count adult takeovers and repeated misunderstandings, and ask enjoyment on a 1–5 scale with one reason. Offer untimed controls and record their use. | At least 80% complete the selected expedition without an adult operating it; median enjoyment at least 4/5. Examine reasons and device differences, not only averages. |
| Age and reading suitability | Ask learners to explain two guide clues and three relevant glossary terms in their own words. Note rereading, skipped paragraphs, and confusing language. | At least 80% can explain the two clues with the available reading supports. Revise language that repeatedly blocks the younger group. |

For a small cohort, show counts alongside percentages. These thresholds are revision triggers, not statistical proof of effectiveness. Do not exclude a struggling learner to improve a score.

## Example transfer prompts

- **Floods:** “A different river briefly connects a meadow pool, then the outlet closes. Does the remaining water area tell you whether fish can complete their seasonal journey? What else matters?”
- **Migration:** “A clean tributary contains suitable nursery habitat behind a new barrier. What observations would show whether a passage helps the intended fish?”
- **Productivity:** “Two coasts lie at similar latitudes, but one receives more nutrient-rich water. What would you predict, and what records could challenge the prediction?”

Use a simple discussion rubric: **0** = no mechanism yet; **1** = a relevant mechanism but weak evidence; **2** = a plausible mechanism with a relevant clue and a stated limitation. Score explanations independently of the in-game first-answer count. Retain the original response when discussing a correction.

## Record and decide

For each participant, retain the setup, before/after/delayed explanation, confusion points, help given, completion status, and enjoyment reason. Exported game records can support the account of what happened, but cannot reveal every thought or establish learning by themselves. The game does not automatically collect this study data.

Revise recurring misunderstandings first. Repeat a small pilot with the revised build and identify its version; do not quietly combine results across materially different versions. A larger and better-controlled study would be needed for a stronger claim about learning gains. Until then, report the pilot honestly, including who struggled, what remains uncertain, and which targets were not met.
